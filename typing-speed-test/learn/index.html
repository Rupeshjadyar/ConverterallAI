Touch Typing Ergonomics & Core Guidelines

1. Floating Wrists

Do not rest your wrists flat against the desk while typing. Keep your forearms parallel to the floor and let your wrists float gently above the keys to avoid carpal compression.

2. Home Row Tactile Nubs

The keys F and J have raised tactile lines. Train your index fingers to find them by feel alone without looking down at the keyboard.

3. Rhythm Beats Speed

Typing at an even, metronomic cadence is far faster than bursting and hesitating. Speed is simply what happens when you stop making errors.

4. Opposite Shift Key Rule

Always use the opposite hand's pinky to press Shift. If the letter is on the left hand (e.g. 'T'), use the Right Shift. This prevents awkward wrist twisting.
