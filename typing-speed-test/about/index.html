Typing Metrics & Mathematics

How WPM, accuracy, and consistency are computed down to each millisecond.

The Standard 5-Character "Word"

In professional typing competitions and ergonomics research, a "word" is standardized as exactly 5 keystrokes (including letters, spaces, and punctuation). This eliminates bias caused by differences in word length: typing five 2-letter words like "in an on at it" (14 characters with spaces) is not rewarded five times higher than typing one 14-letter word like "infrastructure".

Core Metrics & Exact Formulas

Gross WPM = (All Typed Characters / 5) / Time in Minutes
Raw WPM measures your physical keystroke speed regardless of whether errors were committed.
Worked Example: If you type 300 characters in 60 seconds (1.0 minute):
Gross WPM = (300 / 5) / 1.0 = 60 WPM.
Net WPM = (Correct Characters / 5) / Time in Minutes
Net WPM is the true industry benchmark metric. Only accurately typed characters count toward your final score.
Worked Example: In that same 60 seconds, 285 characters were typed correctly and 15 were typos:
Net WPM = (285 / 5) / 1.0 = 57 Net WPM.
Accuracy % = (Correct Keystrokes / Total Keystrokes) × 100
Every physical key strike is audited. If you press a wrong letter and backspace it, both keystrokes factor into the total attempt.
Worked Example: 285 correct strokes out of 300 total physical presses:
Accuracy = (285 / 300) × 100 = 95.0%.
Consistency % = max(0, 100 × (1 - (σ / μ)))
Where σ is the standard deviation of your per-second Net WPM and μ is your mean speed.
Why it matters: A typist who maintains a steady 70 WPM throughout scores ~95% consistency, whereas a typist who bursts at 110 WPM and then stops for 3 seconds to fix a typo scores ~60% consistency. Pacing produces mastery.

Character States & Breakdown

  • Correct (ok): The character matches the expected prompt perfectly.
  • Incorrect (err): The character typed did not match the expected prompt letter.
  • Extra (extra): Letters typed beyond the prompt word length (displayed as red overflow).
  • Missed (miss): Letters left untyped when pressing Space early to advance to the next word.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

Does TypePulse make any external network requests?
None whatsoever. No CDNs, no web fonts, no external APIs, and no tracking scripts are loaded. You can open index.html directly via the file:// protocol in airplane mode without internet.
How are the keyboard switch sounds generated?
Using the browser's built-in Web Audio API. When a key is struck, a mathematical oscillator and bandpass filter shape a microsecond transient pulse mimicking Cherry MX mechanical switches or vintage typewriter hammers.
Where are my test records and stats stored?
In your browser's private localStorage on this computer. You can export a full JSON backup at any time from the Stats page.
What are the skill rankings?
Beginner: <25 WPM • Intermediate: 25–45 WPM • Advanced: 45–70 WPM • Pro: 70–100 WPM • Legend: 100+ WPM.
